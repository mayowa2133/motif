## Motion specification: actual behavior, not an adjective

The numbers here are reconstruction starting values; they are not measured source settings.

1. Layer hierarchy: placement → authored action → local living motion → render geometry. Compose transforms before evaluating attachment points. A held prop follows the final hand pose, not a stale pre-jitter position.
2. Selected cutout living holds: seeded, frame-indexed x/y variations about ±0.6–2 design px and rotation about ±0.5–1.3°. Hold a value for roughly 2–4 output frames or mix that stepped movement with a small smooth wobble. Vary phases. Major impacts can briefly exceed those amplitudes. Not every background/object must jitter.
3. Bot reactions: quick pose/expression swaps; antenna follow-through; 1–3 frames of anticipation before a short jump or stamp; squash to roughly 0.90–0.94 height on landing and rebound through 1.05–1.10. Keep readable proportions and genuine contact. Bigger gestures beat slow elongated reaching arms.
4. Pops: scale from about 0.72–0.9 through 1.08–1.15 to 1 over approximately 7–11 frames. Small caption pops can complete sooner, around 5–8 frames. A state change is not an excuse for every element to pop together.
5. Hops on menu/buttons: explicit launch, arc, contact, result and reaction. Source-like short hops can take 7–12 frames. Button/row highlight follows the landing; earlier particles can still exist when the next hop starts. A floating sprite hop is allowed; a realistic walking rig is unnecessary.
6. Waveforms: 24–50 slim rounded bars, changing heights with an organic envelope, local phase differences, and a separate reveal mask. They keep moving during nearby speech/caption/character activity. Do not substitute one static waveform or identical sine-height bars. Sound ribbon is a separate thick flowing curve, often three or four lobes, with pink body and peach end.
7. UI spinners/rings and counters remain active until completion. Completion replaces—not overlays ambiguously—the loading indicator. Count-up must really change digits over time.
8. Paper flight uses anchored origins, rotation, curved paths, brief overlap and destination response. Loose pages originate from the book, samples from their recording, receipts from billing, and app tiles from the completed CTA.
9. Local starbursts: 4-point white/pink/yellow stars and occasional larger papery burst. Small pieces live roughly 10–22 frames, vary scale/path and fade/settle. They come from the interaction, not the whole canvas.
10. Camera: most interest comes from objects and set changes. Mild set push or drift is allowed where it supports scale, but no continuous seasick camera or arbitrary up/down correction. Preserve named targets/contact; don't reapply scale to already-transformed children.
11. All output is 30 fps. Do not lower the entire film frame rate to imitate stop motion. Quantize selected LOCAL living motion only. Do not sample fresh randomness per render call.
12. Most cuts begin with a complete, already-active interface; never apply one slow enter animation to the whole new world. Begin the next subaction before every prior piece has settled.
