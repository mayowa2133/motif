# Review of the preserved first Little Book Workshop render

Agent inspection of decoded frames at 0.2, 1.4, 2.7, 3.5, 4.5, 5.3, 6.8, 8.7, 9.5, 10.5, 11.4, 12.5, 13.3, 14.1 and 15.5 seconds.

The premise, three-way overlap and same-piece assembly/delivery are present. This is a new metaphor, not an A/B test. Two shared implementation defects were found and repaired before the next run: the grounded floor was outside the prop camera, so the desk floated during reframing; and the page-press contact plate was occluded behind its workpiece. Workshop establish geometry also showed excess empty room and caused an unhelpful vertical jump. The scene now frames the actual workshop and keeps the floor with the furniture. These are agent-assisted geometry and layer-order repairs, not model inventions.

The final narration needs a creative correction. "Three grips press" and "deliver forward; waiting hands take ... full weight" sound like instructions to an animator. The audience is learning the idea of splitting a project, working on independent parts at the same time, then getting one assembled result back. Let the images explain tools, grips, physical travel and contact. Use familiar, natural viewer-facing language about that idea, without verbally certifying every action. Do not copy a supplied example script; create the final script yourself. Keep all four meaningful actions in order, cues early enough for their required durations, and leave only a short closing hold. Approximately 40–45 words fits the measured 16–20 second bound on this machine. No need for verdict headlines. Preserve the chosen book metaphor and participating Bots; this is a refinement of one film.
