# Initial director storyboard

The Reversible Rowhouse Folio: a large, irregular paper rowhouse is altered through a slotted roof mechanism. An unsafe overwrite nearly loses the original roof, so a paper-gold history spine catches it. The Bot then stores the intact house as a keyed checkpoint, installs a visibly different coral roof, performs a physical rain test, observes a leak and sagging floor, and pulls the checkpoint mechanism to restore the original roof and dry interior. A successful repeat test and reverse-folding history trail prove that the whole path—not merely the final object—is reversible.

One persistent tactile hero turns version history into physical evidence. The opening establishes a readable original state and the danger of untracked change; the middle shows checkpointing, comparison and a consequential test; the ending restores the exact opening silhouette and successfully retests it. The rowhouse, roof profiles, water path and sagging floor remain legible without captions at 360×640. All motion is bounded to explicit paper parts, affine transforms, keyed contacts and timed frame events; the single production-specific SVG contains the complete prop system and environmental cues.

## shot-01-unsafe-overwrite

Narration: Giving an AI write access without version history is like letting someone renovate your house with no before photos.

- Establish the intact rowhouse folio from x82–646 and y300–974: a tall cream façade, offset teal pitched roof, three uneven windows and a flat dry floor strip.
- Bot at x132,y824 turns the charcoal write latch, grips the teal roof at its exposed eave anchor and lifts it from two keyed slots.
- A black discard mouth opens behind the roofline; the original roof starts descending while a broad coral replacement roof approaches from the right.
- Bot notices the original roof is about to disappear and arrests the replacement just short of contact, leaving the unsafe overwrite visibly unresolved.

## shot-02-history-catches-change

Narration: Every change should leave a trail.

- Continue the falling motion in a closer view without Bot.
- The paper-gold catch tongue slides under the teal roof and hooks its eave notch before the discard mouth can take it.
- A narrow accordion history strip unfolds from the left rail; its first keyed bay receives the teal roof while its second bay holds the waiting coral roof.
- The discard mouth closes, leaving both ordered roof states physically connected to the house by the gold strip.

## shot-03-save-checkpoint

Narration: Save a checkpoint before the work starts.

- The teal roof returns to both façade slots, restoring the complete dry opening state before the renovation resumes.
- Bot braces the folio and pulls the paper-gold checkpoint tab from the left archive rail.
- A full-height gold silhouette plate travels behind the house; keyed apertures align with the roof, windows and level floor strip.
- The plate slides back into the archive pocket carrying a compact raised relief of the intact configuration, while the live house remains unchanged.

## shot-04-visible-difference

Narration: Show exactly what changed.

- Without Bot, the teal roof lifts from the live house and folds left on the attached history strip while the archived checkpoint remains visible behind it.
- The coral replacement roof slides into the same two keyed slots and locks with a shallow paper compression.
- The folio opens on its center hinge: the gold checkpoint relief and teal pitched roof occupy the left leaf; the live cream façade and coral flat roof occupy the right leaf.
- Both roof baselines align across the hinge, making the lost pitch, added flat deck and open drainage notch visually exact.

## shot-05-rain-test-fails

Narration: Let the agent test the result.

- The folio closes to the live coral-roof house; Bot grips the exposed rain-ribbon anchor above the roof.
- Bot pulls the translucent teal ribbon downward at constant speed, carrying three large droplet cutouts across the coral roof profile.
- The first two droplets pool behind the flat roof lip; the third follows the open notch and enters the upper window.
- On contact, the cream floor strip turns teal from left to right and sags fourteen pixels at its unsupported center.

## shot-06-rollback-restores

Narration: And if the new version is worse, roll back instead of patching over the mistake.

- Bot plants both feet and pulls the paper-gold checkpoint tab through its full marked travel, without touching the leak or adding any patch.
- The linked checkpoint carriage withdraws the coral roof into the right history bay while the teal original roof travels from the archive rail toward the empty façade slots.
- At the carriage midpoint, the wet floor strip rotates once around its concealed paper hinge, presenting its preserved cream dry face.
- The teal roof lands in both keyed slots; the gold checkpoint relief aligns behind the façade and the house returns to its exact opening silhouette.

## shot-07-restored-test-passes

Narration: Good automation doesn’t just move fast.

- With Bot absent, the same translucent rain ribbon descends at the same measured speed and alignment used for the failed test.
- All three droplet cutouts meet the restored teal pitch and travel outward along its overlapping shingle edge.
- The droplets enter the side gutter and slide into a gold catch cup outside the façade.
- The interior floor remains cream and level throughout the complete test window.

## shot-08-every-step-reversible

Narration: It makes every step reversible.

- The attached gold history spine fans across the frame displaying THREE states: original checkpoint, a failed-state bay whose coral roof and wet-test floor are coupled parts, and the restored dry house.
- A gold return tab reverses direction; the wet-test state folds onto the failed roof state, then both fold backward into the checkpoint bay.
- The failed coral roof remains preserved inside the closed history pocket while the live teal roof, windows and dry floor align exactly with the opening composition.
- The gold spine seats beneath the house; the HOUSE roof, windows and dry floor exactly match the opening. Successful-test droplets remain in the exterior cup as retained test evidence, not part of the claimed house-state identity.
