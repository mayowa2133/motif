# Directing questions to test on a new brief

These questions come from separately reviewed, source-informed repairs. They are
hypotheses for an unseen film, not evidence of successful blind transfer. Derive
the scene from the newly admitted script; keep withheld references closed until
the independent verdict is frozen. No previous source shots, props, trajectories
or artwork are supplied by this guide.

## Plan a visible change

- Distinguish physical roles and spaces when narrative function requires it.
  Several sets alone do not establish useful direction; a fixed board can work.
- Show the initial state, operation and changed state. A labeled prop does not
  replace an action, and depicted mechanisms remain illustrations unless verified.
- Retain meaningful results until an explicit replacement, removal or exit.
- Track object identity, holder, material state, support and attached pieces.
- Frame the current actor, stimulus or result and give the payoff a readable hold.
- Complete requirements before the dependent act; test text at delivery size.
  There is no universal reading-duration rule.
- Keep decision, contact and response visible. Brief purposeful occlusion can pass
  if the relevant action remains understood and visibility recovers.
- Explain origins, traversal, linked arrivals, contact, separation and settled state.

When comparing plans, preregister structural differences in spaces, roles,
principal moving entity, operation, framing and payoff. Cosmetic alternatives do
not test a different causal idea. Record the hardest risk and plausible failure;
choose by script priorities. This is an optional evaluation protocol, not a new
production quota or a demonstrated superiority claim.

For each important beat, record script span, scene, actor/holder, object/support,
initial state, operation/contact, changed state, retained result, framing, text
completion, exit and test frames. Where applicable, ask for condition, preparation,
operation, result, reaction and retained consequence; state justified exceptions.

## Review the encoded evidence

Inspect before/during/after each critical operation and every native frame across
risky handoffs, transformations and entrances/exits. Check holder, attachment,
support, readable state and performer visibility. Resolve ambiguous thumbnails
with larger decoded frames rather than treating a pixel-distance threshold as
proof of semantic contact.

At delivery size, independently retrieve the condition before its dependent act
and identify actor, stimulus and result. Include longest text, entering cards,
cut/reframe boundaries and the ending. After a repair, inspect changed intervals
and neighboring anchors in the new encode. Compiled-source equality is not encoded
pixel identity.

Check runtime/layout/contrast, expected frames/dimensions/rate/streams and a full
decode separately from visual comprehension. Record movie hashes and actually
viewed frame IDs. Withhold the producer's rationale/verdict and queued reference
from the independent blind review. Mark full-speed motion, listening and AV sync
unassessed unless actually assessed.

## Preserve the limits

A source-informed reconstruction can demonstrate a local repair without showing
general directing ability. Novel choices need their own evidence. Spectacle can
damage continuity or reading time, and source matching does not invalidate useful
blind choices. Keep corrections, reviewer disagreements and accepted limitations;
they are not targets to copy. Do not average a disputed failure into an artistic
winner or treat technical decoding as a complete film-quality pass.
