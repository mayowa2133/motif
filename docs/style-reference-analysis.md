# Reference analysis — seven short-form videos

Status: phase 0, completed before production concepts. Source clips were supplied by the user and inspected at eight distributed timestamps per clip, with quarter-second sequences around representative motions. The 720 × 1280, 30 fps clips run 28–42 seconds. Observations below describe a visual family, not assets to reproduce. The source footage is intentionally kept outside this repository.

## Construction and visual grammar

1. **Character construction:** small, planar figures are assembled from simple silhouettes, separate limbs and a few accessories. The recurring blocky guide is compact and reads even when partly hidden by charts. Other subject characters change color or costume without adding facial complexity.
2. **Proportions:** heads or face areas occupy a large fraction of character height. Arms and legs are short. An oversize prop may be much larger than a character if it carries the idea.
3. **Expression:** tiny eyes, simple mouths and tilted accessories carry emotion. Poses and scene context do more work than detailed facial rendering.
4. **Recurring asset types:** paper banners, word tiles, cards, signs, shelves, clocks, charts, platform bases, UI screens, stamps, check marks and small guide figures.
5. **Prop simplification:** props retain one or two identifying features, then omit small mechanisms. A clock needs a face and hands; a laptop needs a lid, bezel and screen. Readable iconography wins over realism.
6. **Environments:** rooms are shallow stage sets built from a backdrop, a floor or tabletop, and a few cues such as shelves, wall panels or curtains. A scene need not depict a complete location.
7. **Layering:** backgrounds provide color and texture; midground supports the story object; foreground labels, captions, rails or desks overlap the action. Physical occlusion makes the layers clear.
8. **Material:** matte, fibrous, lightly speckled surfaces suggest printed card or cut paper. Texture is visible in broad areas and subdued behind small type.
9. **Cutout qualities:** slight irregularity, separate shadowed layers, taped labels, pasted cards and hinged-looking limbs convey hand assembly. Edges are not perfectly clinical.
10. **Color:** scenes switch among deep teal, navy, plum, warm tan, brick and pale blue backdrops. A warm paper field and near-black type unify changes. Accent colors are assigned to the narrative object, not sprayed across every surface.
11. **Shadow direction:** narrow contact shadows generally fall below and slightly right of pasted objects. Larger environment shadows are soft and low contrast. Exact virtual lighting is secondary to layer separation.
12. **Edges:** silhouettes remain clean at phone scale; visible white or dark edge slivers occasionally imply cut stock. Outlines are selective, especially around dark screens and signs.
13. **Detail level:** the main metaphor uses few large shapes. Fine text is permitted within a screenshot/UI inset, while the scene-level claim appears separately at display size.
14. **Density:** one primary question or result occupies a frame. Surrounding decoration can be busy in tournament or room scenes, but the focal statistic or action remains isolated.
15. **Perspective:** mostly front-on 2D with shallow overlap and occasional simplified top or side planes. No convincing 3D depth or camera lens effects are required.
16. **Screenshots and UI:** realistic-enough product panels sit inside stylized cards, monitors or windows. Their content is more precise than the surrounding props and can be swapped without redesigning the scene.
17. **Headlines:** short, heavy uppercase statements on a pale horizontal paper banner near the top. It persists across an idea beat and changes with the claim.
18. **Captions:** spoken words appear as separate, low-placed, warm colored paper tiles. The quarter-second samples show words adding or replacing tiles while the headline holds steady.
19. **CTA treatment:** calls to action are represented as a physically selectable button, prompt, panel or object. There is no evidence for a single mandatory end-card format across these clips.
20. **Recurring motion:** rise, drop, slide, bounce, stamp, pop, scale change, stacking, reveal and object handoff. Motion often changes the measurable state of a physical metaphor.
21. **Entrance and exit:** elements travel from frame edges or emerge behind another layer; captions arrive word by word; hard scene changes reset the stage. A brief settle or overshoot gives cutouts weight.
22. **Composition:** title zone at top; largest story object in central bands; small guide may stand at lower left/right; caption zone above a dark lower stage. The center stays readable during rapid changes.
23. **Metaphor patterns:** comparisons become races, columns or podiums; selection becomes a tournament; capacity becomes a container; speed becomes transport; reliability becomes inspection or a safety gate.
24. **Abstract-to-physical translation:** each claim is mapped to an object with observable state: height for score, remaining entrants for elimination, a gauge for capacity, a stamp for approval.
25. **Reusable versus custom:** card shells, captions, backgrounds, windows, character parts, gauges, clocks, stages and transition mechanics are reusable. Brand marks, product UI content, data, and story-specific labels are custom.
26. **Fast without chaotic:** a stable top banner and bottom caption band frame each beat. Only one or two story elements move at a time; scenes cut when the metaphor changes.
27. **Charm:** small imperfect motions, tiny arms, expressive accessory tilt, tactile collisions and scale contrasts. The guide can react to a large prop without dominating it.
28. **Asset reuse:** the same guide, banner logic, room cues, caption tiles and chart grammar recur with different labels and colors. Reuse is structural rather than repeating a whole composition.

## Motion evidence from tighter samples

- `Dd2UAqOvDoO`, 6.00–8.75 s: a comparison column grows into place, character and label settle on top, and captions build one phrase at a time. The backdrop and headline remain fixed through the sequence.
- `Dd4Le3YhCQt`, 16.00–18.75 s: a crowded tournament field collapses in several fast elimination steps while the counter changes 50 → 25 → 13 → 7 → 4 → 2 → 1. The spatial metaphor makes the numbers understandable before a viewer reads the caption.
- `Dd45WmVPOfQ`, 14.00–16.75 s: small code objects, status cards and the character change position while the workbench stays fixed; the scene then changes backdrop while preserving the comparison idea.

## Direction for Motif

Borrow the **grammar**: matte cut paper, shallow stage space, modular physical metaphors, clear information hierarchy, and short weighted motions. Build an original identity around the supplied cream-and-teal robot. The reference guide's square orange silhouette, exact taped headline, brick word tiles, brand/UI copies and scene layouts are source-specific and must not be reused as Motif assets.

## Confidence and limits

The visual and sampled-motion observations are grounded in the supplied files. Sound design, exact easing curves, full cut count, font names and material production methods were not measured. Rules in the style bible are Motif design decisions informed by these observations, not claims about the source creators' specifications.
